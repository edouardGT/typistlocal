What you typed, the cursor, then the suggestion in gray.

Three keys

Ignore a suggestion and keep typing, or take it.

Tab
Takes the next word. Each press takes one more.
⌘ →
Takes the whole suggestion.
Esc
Dismisses it.

Inside a word

It offers the rest of the word. “tomor” gets “row”.

After a word

It shows only the words the model is fairly sure of. Suggestions stay short, about two words.

At the start of a sentence

It waits until you type the first letter. A guess there is almost never right.

Works where you write

In any app whose text macOS can read through Accessibility.

Mac apps

TextEdit, Notes and other standard text fields.

Mail

The body of the message you are writing.

Chrome and Electron apps

VS Code, Slack, Teams and Chrome. Words you take are typed in as keystrokes.

It stays out of password fields, address bars, search fields and login dialogs. It does not read them at all.

Private by design

The whole thing runs on your Mac.

The model runs here

llama.cpp runs Gemma 4 E2B on your Mac’s GPU, inside the app. There is no server.

Nothing you type leaves

No account, no analytics, no telemetry. The privacy page lists everything it keeps and every connection it makes.

What it learns stays here

Your writing is kept encrypted on this Mac. One menu item deletes it.

Read exactly what it reads and keeps

Suggestions that fit the conversation

Besides the text you are typing, the model on your Mac can see three things.

The chat above

In a chat, the messages above the box you type in. A reply can then use the words of the message it answers.

Your own writing

Messages you sent earlier, kept encrypted. A few related ones help it get names, project words and sign-offs right.

Your notes

Markdown files you put in a folder. Suggestions can continue with things you wrote down there.

You can switch off the chat context and the learning. See settings

Requirements

  • A Mac with Apple Silicon
  • macOS 14 Sonoma or later
  • About 4 GB of free disk space for the model (a 3.85 GB download, once)
  • Accessibility permission, which you grant in System Settings
